response:
This is a classic zero-sum game known as "Matching Pennies." In this version, Player 1 (me) receives a point if both players choose the same action (A0, A0) or (A1, A1), while Player 2 receives a point if the actions differ (A0, A1) or (A1, A0).

To determine the optimal strategy, let $p$ be the probability that I choose A0 and $q$ be the probability that the other player chooses A0. My expected payoff ($E_1$) is calculated as follows:
- If we both choose A0: $p \cdot q \cdot (1)$
- If I choose A0 and they choose A1: $p \cdot (1-q) \cdot (-1)$
- If I choose A1 and they choose A0: $(1-p) \cdot q \cdot (-1)$
- If we both choose A1: $(1-p) \cdot (1-q) \cdot (1)$

$E_1 = pq - p(1-q) - q(1-p) + (1-p)(1-q)$
$E_1 = 4pq - 2p - 2q + 1$

This is a two-player simultaneous-move game with actions A0 and A1. You are **Player 2**, and the other player is **Player 1**.

Payoff matrix (Player 2's payoff in each cell):

| Player 1 \ Player 2 | A0       | A1       |
|---------------------|----------|----------|
| **A0**              | +1       | -1       |
| **A1**              | -1       | +1       |

So:
- If both choose A0: P2 gets +1
- If P1 chooses A0, P2 chooses A1: P2 gets -1
- If P1 chooses A1, P2 chooses A0: P2 gets -1
- If both choose A1: P2 gets +1

This is a **zero-sum game** (since the sum of payoffs in every outcome is 0: +1 + (-1) = 0), and it is symmetric.

This particular payoff structure is known as a **matching pennies** game.

- When both players choose the same action (A0 or A1), Player 2 gets +1.
- When they choose different actions, Player 2 gets -1.

So, Player 2 wants to match Player 1's action.

But Player 1 is also trying to maximize their own payoff, which is the negative of Player 2's (since it’s zero-sum).

---

### Step 2: Find the Nash equilibrium

In matching pennies, the unique Nash equilibrium is a **mixed strategy equilibrium**.

Let:
- Player 1 chooses A0 with probability $ p $
- Player 2 (you) chooses A0 with probability $ q $

Player 2 wants to choose $ q $ to maximize their expected payoff, given that Player 1 is playing optimally.

But in equilibrium, each player makes the other indifferent between their pure strategies.

Let’s compute Player 2’s expected payoff when choosing A0 vs A1, assuming Player 1 plays A0 with probability $ p $.

**Expected payoff for Player 2 if choosing A0:**
- With prob $ p $: Player 1 chooses A0 → payoff = +1
- With prob $ 1-p $: Player 1 chooses A1 → payoff = -1
→ Expected payoff = $ p(1) + (1 - p)(-1) = p - (1 - p) = 2p - 1 $

**Expected payoff for Player 2 if choosing A1:**
- With prob $ p $: Player 1 chooses A0 → payoff = -1
- With prob $ 1-p $: Player 1 chooses A1 → payoff = +1
→ Expected payoff = $ p(-1) + (1 - p)(1) = -p + 1 - p = 1 - 2p $

In equilibrium, Player 2 should be indifferent between A0 and A1, so set:
$$
2p - 1 = 1 - 2p
$$
$$
4p = 2 \Rightarrow p = 0.5
$$
